# Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Management Protocol (Synthetic)

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT. Not medical guidance. Numbers are illustrative
> and simplified for a software demo. Do not use for real patient care.

## Staging and targets
- CKD is staged by eGFR (G1 >= 90 down to G5 < 15 mL/min/1.73m2) and by
  albuminuria. Lower eGFR and higher albuminuria indicate more advanced disease.
- A common blood-pressure target in CKD is < 130/80 mmHg when tolerated.
- The goal is to slow progression and avoid acute insults, not to "normalize" a
  single value; targets are individualized by a clinician.

## Escalation / urgent review criteria
- Potassium >= 6.0 mmol/L is severe hyperkalemia and requires SAME-DAY / urgent
  clinician review (consider emergency assessment); potassium 5.5-5.9 mmol/L
  warrants same-day clinician review.
- An acute fall in eGFR of >= 25% from the patient's recent baseline, or a new
  drop below 30 mL/min/1.73m2, warrants prompt clinician / nephrology review.
- eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m2 (kidney-failure range) requires urgent nephrology review.
- New oliguria, signs of fluid overload, or uremic symptoms require urgent escalation
  regardless of the numeric values.

## Management guidance (clinician-directed)
- RAAS agents (ACE inhibitor or ARB) are often used in CKD with albuminuria, but
  require monitoring of potassium and eGFR. Hold up-titration if potassium > 5.0
  mmol/L, and do NOT combine an ACE inhibitor and an ARB.
- An SGLT2 inhibitor may be added for kidney and cardiovascular protection in
  eligible patients, at clinician discretion.
- Review and dose-adjust renally cleared medications (e.g., metformin: review at
  eGFR < 45, avoid initiation if eGFR < 30). Avoid nephrotoxins (e.g., NSAIDs) and
  hold them around contrast or acute illness.
- An automated tool must NOT start, stop, or dose these agents on its own; it
  surfaces options and flags for clinician review.

## Monitoring
- Recheck eGFR and potassium after any RAAS change (typically within 1-2 weeks)
  and periodically thereafter, more frequently at lower eGFR.
- Track albuminuria and blood pressure over time as markers of progression.
