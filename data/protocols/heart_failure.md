# Heart Failure (HFrEF) Management Protocol (Synthetic)

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT. Not medical guidance. Numbers are illustrative
> and simplified for a software demo. Do not use for real patient care.

## Monitoring signals
- Daily weights are a core monitoring tool. A weight gain of >= 2 kg over 3 days,
  or >= 2.3 kg (about 5 lb) over a week, suggests fluid retention and warrants
  clinician review for possible diuretic adjustment.
- Falling oxygen saturation (SpO2 < 92%) with weight gain or breathlessness
  suggests decompensation and requires SAME-DAY clinician review.

## Guideline-directed medical therapy (clinician-directed)
- Four pillars: beta-blocker (e.g., carvedilol), ARNI or ACE inhibitor/ARB
  (e.g., sacubitril-valsartan), MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor.
- Up-titrate toward target doses as blood pressure, heart rate, potassium, and
  renal function allow.
- Hold or reduce diuretic only with clinician direction; do not stop a beta-blocker
  abruptly during decompensation.

## Escalation / urgent review criteria
- Rapid weight gain (>= 2 kg / 3 days) AND a new drop in SpO2 -> SAME-DAY review.
- SpO2 < 90%, resting breathlessness, or new orthopnea -> urgent escalation.
- Symptomatic hypotension (SBP < 90 mmHg with dizziness) -> urgent escalation.

## Notes
- NT-proBNP trends can support assessment but should be interpreted by a clinician
  in the context of renal function and the patient's baseline.
